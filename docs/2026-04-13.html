The following is a list of common errors in English writing and how to correct them.


1. Subject-Verb Agreement:

* Incorrect: The dog bark at the cat.

* Correct: The dog barks at the cat.

* Explanation: Singular subjects take singular verbs (e.g., "barks"), and plural subjects take plural verbs (e.g., "bark").


2. Pronoun Agreement:

* Incorrect: Everyone should bring their own lunch.

* Correct: Everyone should bring his or her own lunch. (or "their own lunch" if gender-neutrality is preferred and context allows)

* Explanation: Pronouns must agree in number and gender with the nouns they refer to. "Everyone" is singular, so it should be followed by a singular pronoun.


3. Run-on Sentences:

* Incorrect: I went to the store I bought some milk.

* Correct: I went to the store, and I bought some milk. (or "I went to the store; I bought some milk.")

* Explanation: A run-on sentence combines two or more independent clauses without proper punctuation or a coordinating conjunction.


4. Comma Splices:

* Incorrect: The weather was cold, I wore a heavy coat.

* Correct: The weather was cold, so I wore a heavy coat. (or "The weather was cold; I wore a heavy coat.")

* Explanation: A comma splice occurs when two independent clauses are joined by only a comma.


5. Fragment Sentences:

* Incorrect: Running quickly down the street.

* Correct: He was running quickly down the street.

* Explanation: A fragment sentence is an incomplete sentence that lacks a subject, a verb, or both, or does not express a complete thought.


6. Misplaced Modifiers:

* Incorrect: I saw a dog walking down the street with a broken leg.

* Correct: I saw a dog with a broken leg walking down the street.

* Explanation: A misplaced modifier is a word or phrase that modifies an unintended word because of its placement in the sentence.


7. Dangling Modifiers:

* Incorrect: Walking through the park, the trees were beautiful.

* Correct: Walking through the park, I saw beautiful trees.

* Explanation: A dangling modifier is a word or phrase that modifies a word not clearly stated in the sentence.


8. Apostrophe Usage (Possessives vs. Plurals):

* Incorrect: The dog's are barking.

* Correct: The dogs are barking. (Plural)

* Incorrect: The students books.

* Correct: The students' books. (Possessive)

* Explanation: Apostrophes are used for possessives (e.g., "dog's bone") and contractions (e.g., "it's"), not for making nouns plural.


9. Homophone Confusion:

* Incorrect: Their going to the store.

* Correct: They're going to the store.

* Explanation: Homophones are words that sound alike but have different meanings and spellings (e.g., "their," "there," "they're").


10. Tense Consistency:

* Incorrect: She walked to the store and buys some groceries.

* Correct: She walked to the store and bought some groceries.

* Explanation: Maintain a consistent verb tense throughout a sentence or paragraph unless there is a clear reason to shift tenses.


11. Parallelism:

* Incorrect: I like to swim, to hike, and reading.

* Correct: I like to swim, to hike, and to read. (or "I like swimming, hiking, and reading.")

* Explanation: When listing items or ideas, they should be presented in the same grammatical form.


12. Wordiness/Redundancy:

* Incorrect: In my opinion, I think that the project is good.

* Correct: I think the project is good. (or "In my opinion, the project is good.")

* Explanation: Avoid unnecessary words or phrases that do not add new meaning.


13. Article Usage (a/an/the):

* Incorrect: I want a apple.

* Correct: I want an apple.

* Explanation: Use "a" before consonant sounds and "an" before vowel sounds. Use "the" for specific nouns.


14. Preposition Usage:

* Incorrect: I am good in math.

* Correct: I am good at math.

* Explanation: Prepositions often follow specific verbs, adjectives, or nouns. Learning common collocations is key.


15. Comma Usage (Introductory Phrases/Clauses):

* Incorrect: After the meeting we went to lunch.

* Correct: After the meeting, we went to lunch.

* Explanation: Use a comma after an introductory phrase or clause to separate it from the main clause.


16. Semicolon Usage:

* Incorrect: I like coffee, I also like tea.

* Correct: I like coffee; I also like tea.

* Explanation: Semicolons can join two closely related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction. They can also be used in complex lists.


17. Colon Usage:

* Incorrect: I need to buy milk, eggs, and bread.

* Correct: I need to buy the following: milk, eggs, and bread.

* Explanation: Colons are used to introduce a list, an explanation, or a quotation.


18. Hyphen Usage (Compound Adjectives):

* Incorrect: He is a well known author.

* Correct: He is a well-known author.

* Explanation: Use a hyphen to connect words that form a single adjective before a noun (e.g., "long-term goal").


19. Capitalization:

* Incorrect: i went to new york.

* Correct: I went to New York.

* Explanation: Capitalize the first word of a sentence, proper nouns (names, places, specific titles), and the pronoun "I."


20. Sentence Structure (Clarity and Conciseness):

* Incorrect: The reason why I am late is because I missed the bus.

* Correct: I am late because I missed the bus.

* Explanation: Avoid redundant phrasing and strive for clear, concise sentences.
