알렉스|en|And I'm Alex, your English coach. This is episode 2026-W31.
민지|en|You had three lessons this week — healthcare systems, stress management, and medication.
알렉스|en|Great topics. And you had some really interesting mistakes. Not bad ones. Interesting ones.
민지|en|Because interesting mistakes teach us the most. Today we will not just list what went wrong.
알렉스|en|We will understand why. And when you understand why, the fix becomes natural.
민지|en|So let's start with the big one. The pattern that has appeared seven times now. Subject-Verb Agreement.
알렉스|en|Seven times. That's our headline this week. Minji, what exactly is subject-verb agreement?
민지|en|It means the verb must match the subject. He goes, not he go. She is, not she are.
알렉스|en|Right. And here is the key fact. Korean does not do this. Not at all.
민지|ko|한국어는 주어가 바뀌어도 동사 형태가 변하지 않아요. '나는 가요', '그는 가요', 둘 다 '가요'죠.
알렉스|en|But English keeps a strict rule. He, she, it — the verb changes. You add an S for simple present.
민지|en|He walks. She talks. It works. Always that S for he, she, it.
알렉스|en|Now, let's look at what you actually said this week.
민지|en|You said: I'm not agree that.
알렉스|en|The natural version: I don't agree with that.
민지|en|Two things happened there. Can you spot them, Alex?
알렉스|en|Sure. First, "agree" is a verb, not an adjective. You cannot say "I'm agree" just like you cannot say "I'm like" or "I'm want."
민지|ko|'동의해요'를 '나는 동의 상태야'처럼 느껴서 "I'm agree"가 나오기 쉬워요. 하지만 agree는 동사라서 "I agree"라고 직접 써야 해요.
알렉스|en|Second: the preposition. "I don't agree WITH that." Not "agree that." You agree with something.
민지|en|Rule of thumb: if it's something you DO — agree, understand, think, like — just use the verb directly.
알렉스|en|I agree. I understand. I think. I want. Simple and clean.
민지|en|No I'm agree, no I'm understand, no I'm want.
알렉스|en|Now let's look at a longer sentence. This one has a lot going on.
민지|en|You said: The main reason of stress is the human relationship because every stress caused from the humans.
알렉스|en|Let's take this apart piece by piece.
민지|en|First: "reason of" is not natural. We say "reason FOR." The reason for stress. The reason for the problem.
알렉스|ko|한국어는 '스트레스의 이유'처럼 '의'를 쓰지만, 영어는 reason for 이에요. of 가 아니라요.
알렉스|en|Second: "the human relationship" should be "human relationships" — plural.
민지|en|When we talk about human relationships in general, we use the plural. Not one specific relationship.
알렉스|en|Third: "every stress caused from" — two problems. "Stress" is uncountable. Use "all stress" not "every stress."
민지|en|And we say "all stress originates FROM" or "is caused BY." The prepositions are different.
알렉스|en|The full natural sentence: The main reason for stress is human relationships because all stress originates from people.
민지|en|Say that once out loud. Seriously, it helps.
알렉스|en|Okay. Let's shift to the Correction Clinic. We go fast here. Try to repeat each one after us.
민지|en|You said: Every wintertime, we produce the mandarin from the Jeju Island.
알렉스|en|Better: Every winter, we grow mandarins on Jeju Island.
민지|en|"Every winter" sounds more natural than "every wintertime." And Jeju Island does not need "the" — it is a proper name.
알렉스|en|Also: "mandarins" is plural because we mean the fruit in general, not one specific fruit.
민지|en|You said: The first I consider is it possible to go to the airport or not?
알렉스|en|Better: First, I consider whether it's possible to go to the airport or not.
민지|en|"The first I consider" is missing something — a noun. You can say "The first thing I consider."
알렉스|en|Or just say "First, I consider." Clean and direct. And "whether" is the natural connector for yes-or-no questions.
민지|en|Whether it's possible. Whether she can come. Whether or not. Very useful pattern.
알렉스|en|You said: So, AI also has the power relation and some bad information.
민지|en|Better: So, AI can also have biases and provide misleading information.
알렉스|en|"Power relation" is not standard here. "Biases" means unfair preferences — when a system or source leans one way.
민지|en|And "misleading" is a great word. It means something that makes you believe something wrong.
알렉스|en|You said: Your sound is far away. It's good?
민지|en|Better: Your sound is a bit faint. Is it better now?
알렉스|en|"Far away" talks about distance. For sound, we say "faint" or "quiet." Faint means you can barely hear it.
민지|en|And "Is it better now?" is more polite and natural than "It's good?" — which sounds a bit too blunt.
알렉스|en|Two more. You said: I don't know why that paragraph divides all kinds of the regions.
민지|en|Better: I don't know why that paragraph categorizes things by region.
알렉스|en|"Categorizes by region" is cleaner and more precise than "divides all kinds of the regions."
민지|en|And: I think we will first check the package instruction.
알렉스|en|Better: I think we first check the package instructions. Plural. One medicine box has many instructions.
민지|en|Great clinic. Now let's go to Vocabulary in Context. Words need to live in sentences, not just definitions.
알렉스|en|First word: cultivation. Noun. Growing plants or crops for food or other use.
민지|en|Real sentence: Jeju Island has a long history of mandarin cultivation.
알렉스|en|Collocations: land under cultivation, cultivation of crops, cultivation methods.
민지|en|Next: feasibility. Noun. Whether something is possible and practical.
알렉스|en|Real sentence: Before investing, the company did a full feasibility study.
민지|en|"Feasibility study" is a phrase you will hear a lot in business and engineering contexts.
알렉스|en|Next: bias. Noun. An unfair preference for or against something.
민지|en|Real sentence: We need to watch for bias in AI-generated content.
알렉스|en|Related words: biased means not neutral. Unbiased means fair and balanced.
민지|en|Next: disseminate. Verb. To spread information widely.
알렉스|en|Real sentence: Health authorities disseminate guidelines to help people stay safe.
민지|en|It's a formal word. In everyday speech you might say "spread" or "share." But in writing, disseminate sounds strong.
알렉스|en|Next: remedy. Noun or verb. A solution or treatment.
민지|en|As a noun: A warm ginger drink is a common home remedy for a sore throat.
알렉스|en|As a verb: We need to remedy this situation quickly before it gets worse.
민지|en|And last: prevalent. Adjective. Widespread and common.
알렉스|en|Real sentence: Stress is increasingly prevalent among office workers in big cities.
민지|en|Now our Topic Talk segment. The theme this week was health. So here are five useful health phrases at A2-B1 level.
알렉스|en|One: come down with something. To get sick.
민지|en|Example: I came down with a bad cold on Monday. I had to rest all day.
알렉스|en|Two: be under the weather. To feel unwell.
민지|en|Example: She is a bit under the weather today. She might leave work early.
알렉스|en|Three: describe your symptoms. Very useful at a clinic or pharmacy.
민지|en|Example: The doctor asked me to describe my symptoms before giving me medicine.
알렉스|en|Four: follow the instructions. For medicine and treatments.
민지|en|Example: Always follow the package instructions. Do not take more than the recommended amount.
알렉스|en|Five: check in with your doctor. To talk to a doctor for advice, even briefly.
민지|en|Example: If the pain continues, check in with your doctor before taking anything else.

민지|en|It comes from Italian. The word is "quarantina." And it means forty.
알렉스|en|Forty? Why forty?

알렉스|en|And in those days, the Black Death — a terrible plague — was spreading fast across Europe.
민지|en|So Venice decided: any ship arriving at our port must wait forty days before anyone can come ashore.
알렉스|en|Forty days offshore. No contact with land. This was to check if anyone on the ship was sick.

민지|en|The word quarantine has been part of medicine ever since. And in 2020, the whole world suddenly remembered it.
알렉스|en|Language carries history. That is what makes it fascinating.
