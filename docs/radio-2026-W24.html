민지|en|Hello and welcome back to your Weekly English Radio, the show where your real mistakes become your best teachers.
알렉스|en|I am Alex, your English coach, and beside me is the wonderful 민지.
민지|en|This is week 2026-W24, and our learner finished five lessons this week.
알렉스|en|Five lessons, seventeen in total now, with a fluency score of about 7.2.
민지|ko|오늘은 실수를 나열만 하지 말고, 왜 그런지 이해해 봐요.
알렉스|en|Exactly. We do not just list errors here. We understand them.
민지|en|This week our learner talked about big, real topics.
알렉스|en|Nursing homes and retirement in Korea, stress at work, grocery shopping, high-stress jobs, and buying clothes.
민지|en|Such grown-up, thoughtful topics. I love it.
알렉스|en|And the mistakes that came out are very normal Korean-speaker mistakes. Let us dig in.
민지|en|Alex, what is our number one weakness this week?
알렉스|en|The top pattern is Subject-Verb Agreement. It appeared three times.
민지|en|Subject-Verb Agreement. That little "s" at the end of the verb.
알렉스|en|Yes. Our learner said, He STARE at the pile of DOCUMENT.
민지|en|And the natural version?
알렉스|en|He STARES at the pile of DOCUMENTS. Two small s sounds.
민지|en|Another one was, It DEPEND on the project.
알렉스|en|Right, it should be, It DEPENDS on the project.
민지|en|Why does this happen so often for Korean speakers?
알렉스|en|Great question. In Korean, the verb does not change when the subject changes.
민지|ko|한국어는 주어가 he든 they든 동사 모양이 똑같아서, 영어의 -s 습관이 낯설어요.
알렉스|en|In English, with he, she, or it, we add an s to the verb in the present tense.
민지|en|So I work, you work, but he works.
알렉스|en|Perfect. The third person singular gets the s. It feels strange because Korean has no such rule.
민지|en|Give us a rule of thumb to remember it.
알렉스|en|Here it is. He, she, it - one extra s. Say it like a little song.
민지|ko|기억하세요. he, she, it 다음엔 동사에 s 하나 더.
알렉스|en|And here is a thinking tip. When you talk about one other person or thing, listen for that s.
민지|en|Our second big weakness is tied with two others, Alex.
알렉스|en|Yes, Verb Forms and Auxiliaries appeared three times too.
민지|en|Like the sentence, I went to bed late so it is tired.
알렉스|en|The better version is, I went to bed late so I AM tired.
민지|en|Ah, the person is tired, not "it".
알렉스|ko|한국어는 주어를 자주 생략해서, 영어에서 주어를 빠뜨리거나 it 으로 바꾸기 쉬워요.
알렉스|en|In English we almost always need a clear subject. Who is tired? I am tired.
민지|en|That is such a useful reminder. English loves a clear subject.
알렉스|en|And one more pattern tied for the top - Articles, a, an, and the. Also three times.
민지|en|Articles are so tiny but so tricky.
알렉스|ko|한국어엔 a, an, the 같은 관사가 없어서, 언제 붙이고 빼는지 늘 헷갈려요.
알렉스|en|Right. Korean has no articles, so this is brand new thinking. We will see it again in the clinic.
민지|en|Okay, let us move into our Correction Clinic. Repeat after us, listeners.
알렉스|en|Our learner said, This weekend, it is normal and special.
민지|en|The natural version. This weekend was both normal and special.
알렉스|en|Notice the word "both". It nicely links two ideas, normal and special.
민지|en|Next, The art museum is about the picture.
알렉스|en|More natural. The art museum features paintings and art.
민지|en|The verb "features" sounds so professional there.
알렉스|en|Here is a tense one. This day is the front of summer.
민지|en|And the beautiful natural version. This day marks the beginning of summer.
알렉스|en|"Marks the beginning" - that is a lovely, natural collocation.
민지|en|Our learner also said, it is more far.
알렉스|en|The correct form is, it is FARTHER. Far becomes farther for distance.
민지|ko|far 의 비교급은 more far 가 아니라 farther 예요. 불규칙이라 외워야 해요.
민지|en|Another one. So if you come in Korea, every tree leaves a vivid green color.
알렉스|en|Natural. If you come TO Korea, you will see all the trees have vivid green leaves.
민지|en|"Come to Korea", with "to". Such a small word, big difference.
알렉스|en|Here is one about online shopping. I don't like the online because it is not fun.
민지|en|Better. I don't really enjoy online shopping because it's not fun.
알렉스|en|"I don't really enjoy" sounds softer and very natural in English.
민지|en|And a sweet, curious one. Do you have the season of the rain season?
알렉스|en|Natural. Do you have a rainy season in your country?
민지|en|"A rainy season" - and we drop the doubled word "season".
알렉스|en|One more. I share my time with my family.
민지|en|The natural choice. I spend time with my family.
알렉스|ko|"share time" 보다 "spend time with family" 가 영어권에서 훨씬 자연스러워요.
민지|en|Now it is time for the Tutor's Desk, the corrections from the live lesson.
알렉스|en|These came straight from our learner's real tutor. They are gold.
민지|en|The tutor caught, What stress might the man be PACING?
알렉스|en|And gently fixed it to, What stress might the man be FACING?
민지|en|Pacing and facing. One little sound changes everything.
알렉스|en|Pronunciation matters. "Facing a problem" means dealing with it.
민지|en|The tutor also heard, DEY TINK there will be high stress.
알렉스|en|And corrected it to, THEY THINK there will be high stress.
민지|ko|한국어엔 "th" 발음이 없어서 d 나 t 로 나오기 쉬워요. 혀를 살짝 물어요.
알렉스|en|Put your tongue softly between your teeth for "th". Think, they, there.
민지|en|Another tutor note. A middle aged mans check the grocery product.
알렉스|en|Fixed to, A middle-aged man checks the grocery item.
민지|en|There is our friend again - man, not mans, and checks with an s.
알렉스|en|The tutor also fixed plurals. Other womens became other women.
민지|en|And business mans became businessmen. English plurals can be irregular.
알렉스|en|And a clothing one. Usually two time per years became, Usually two times per year.
민지|en|Times is plural, but year stays singular after "per". Tricky little balance.
알렉스|en|The tutor also reshaped, white expert nursing clothes into, a white nursing uniform.
민지|en|"Uniform" is the exact word. So much cleaner.
알렉스|en|And a fun pronunciation note. The tutor heard "result" as REE-ZERT.
민지|ko|result 의 s 는 부드러운 s 소리예요. "리절트", z 가 아니에요.
알렉스|en|Say it softly. Result. Not ree-zert.
민지|en|These tutor notes are precious because they come from real, live speaking.

민지|en|Our first word is "productive". It means useful and full of results.
알렉스|en|Collocation. I had a very productive day at work.
민지|en|Next, "peaceful". Calm and quiet.
알렉스|en|A peaceful retirement in the countryside. Doesn't that sound nice?

알렉스|en|I envision myself living near the sea when I am older.

알렉스|en|My job offers a lot of flexibility, so I can manage my time.

민지|en|"Provisions" - food supplies, often a large amount.
알렉스|en|And "time constraints". I shop online because of time constraints.
민지|ko|time constraints 는 "시간 제약" 이에요. 바쁜 일상에 딱 맞는 표현이죠.
민지|en|A few more from stress and weather. "Incentive", "meditation", "typhoon".
알렉스|en|The government gave an incentive. I practice meditation. A typhoon is coming.
민지|en|Wonderful. Now, my favorite part - the Culture Corner.
알렉스|en|Today, the secret history of English words. Where words come from.
민지|en|Tell us, why is the animal a "cow" but the food is "beef"?
알렉스|en|Great mystery. Long ago, in 1066, the French conquered England.
민지|en|The French ruled, and the English farmers worked the land.
알렉스|en|So the farmer, speaking English, called the animal a cow, a pig, a sheep.
민지|en|And the rich French rulers at the dinner table?
알렉스|en|They used French words. Beef from boeuf, pork from porc, mutton from mouton.
민지|ko|동물은 영국 농부의 단어 cow, 식탁 위 고기는 프랑스 지배층의 단어 beef 가 된 거예요.
민지|en|So every time we say "beef", we are hearing history. Amazing.
알렉스|en|And here is a darker one. The word "deadline".
민지|en|Deadline. We all fear it. Where did it come from?
알렉스|en|Long ago, in a prison camp, there was a line on the ground.
민지|en|A line?
알렉스|en|Yes. If a prisoner crossed that line, the guards would shoot. It was the "dead line".
민지|ko|deadline 은 원래 감옥에서 넘으면 총 맞는 "죽음의 선" 이었어요. 그래서 무서운 단어죠.
민지|en|So next time you have a deadline, at least no one is shooting.
알렉스|en|Exactly. Words carry tiny, surprising stories inside them.
민지|en|That is why learning English is like being a detective.
