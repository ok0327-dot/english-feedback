민지|en|Welcome back to Weekly English Radio. I am Minji, your host. So glad you are here today.
알렉스|en|And I am Alex, your English coach. This is week 39 of 2026 — two lessons from September 21 to 23.
민지|en|Two big real-life topics this week: autumn around the world, and Korean drinking culture.
알렉스|en|Real conversations, real vocabulary. Exactly the kind of English you need outside the classroom.
민지|en|And today we are finding the ROOT of each correction — not just the surface fix, but the reason behind it.
알렉스|en|Understanding the root is what makes a correction permanent. You stop guessing and start knowing.
민지|en|Ready? Let us dig in. The big root this week: the helper verb, also called the auxiliary verb.
알렉스|en|In English, verbs almost always travel in pairs. One carries the main meaning. The other marks time, status, or possibility.
민지|en|Korean does not use this pair system. One verb in Korean does everything. So the helper keeps disappearing.
알렉스|en|And when the helper disappears, the sentence sounds incomplete to a native ear — even if the basic meaning comes through.
민지|ko|한국어는 동사 하나로 시제, 진행, 가능성을 모두 담아요. 영어의 조동사(helper)는 한국어 습관에서 눈에 안 보여 자꾸 빠지는 거예요.
알렉스|en|Example one from this week. You said: Three years ago I working at the sales team.
민지|en|Natural version: Three years ago I was working on the sales team.
알렉스|en|The missing helper is WAS. WORKING alone in past tense cannot stand. It absolutely needs WAS in front.
민지|en|Think of a music duo. WAS is the lead singer. WORKING is the guitarist. The duo cannot perform with only one person on stage.
알렉스|en|Full pattern: was or were, then the -ing form. I was working. She was studying. They were laughing. He was cooking dinner when I called.
민지|en|Note also: not at the sales team — use on the sales team. You are ON the team, always.
알렉스|en|On the marketing team. On the design team. On the sales team. Always on.
민지|en|Example two. You said: I can found some train tickets.
알렉스|en|Natural version: I can find some train tickets. Or if it already happened: I found some train tickets.
민지|en|Rule: after any modal verb — can, will, should, must, could, would — always use the BASE FORM.
알렉스|en|Base form is the clean, original dictionary verb. No -ed. No -ing. No -s. Just the plain verb itself.
민지|en|Can find. Will go. Should try. Must be. Could come. Would stay. Modal plus base form, every time.
알렉스|en|Quick drill. I give the start, you say the right form.
민지|en|She will... go. Not goes, not went. Base form: go.
알렉스|en|You should... call. Not called, not calling. Base form: call.
민지|en|He might... arrive. Not arrives, not arrived. Base form: arrive.
알렉스|en|They could... stay. Not stayed, not staying. Base form: stay.
민지|en|Modal plus base form. No exceptions, no guessing. Lock it in.
알렉스|en|Third pattern: subject-verb agreement, especially with plural nouns.
민지|en|The sentence from this week: These day many beer or soju company is destructions.
알렉스|en|Three things to fix. First: these day — should be these days. These and days are both plural. They must match.
민지|en|Second: company is — you said many companies. Plural subject takes plural verb. Many companies ARE.
알렉스|en|Third: destructions is not natural. Say: companies are closing, shutting down, going out of business.
민지|ko|영어는 주어가 복수면 동사도 are 같은 복수 형태로 맞춰야 해요. 한국어에 이 규칙이 없어서 단수 동사가 자꾸 나와요.
알렉스|en|Before is or are, ask — one thing or many things? One company is. Many companies are. One second, big impact.
민지|en|Quick articles reminder: articles are your cumulative top weakness — twelve times since May.
알렉스|en|Seasons with context almost always take the. The autumn was beautiful. The summer is ending. The winter is coming.
민지|en|Because when we say the autumn in your country, we both know which one — the current one. Shared knowledge means the.
알렉스|en|New, first-mention gets a or an. General categories get nothing. Water is important. But the water in this glass tonight gets the.
민지|en|Quick fix on comparatives: usually alcohol is the more low. Natural: usually alcohol is lower.
알렉스|en|Short adjectives add -er. Low becomes lower. High becomes higher. Long adjectives use more in front.
민지|en|Correction Clinic now. Say the better version out loud after me.
알렉스|en|You said: Maybe how about your country's beauty?
민지|en|Better: What about the autumn in your country? How about and what about both work for questions and suggestions.
알렉스|en|You said: my bag is old, almost three years more past.
민지|en|Better: it has been over three years. Still true now — use present perfect.
알렉스|en|You said: I cannot win through my desire.
민지|en|Better: I cannot resist my cravings. Win through is Korean. English uses resist a craving, overcome a fear.
알렉스|en|You said: Today's topic is what you go to drink, right?
민지|en|Better: Today's topic is about go-to drinks. A topic is always ABOUT something.
알렉스|en|You said: I was wondering about go-to is the usual expression.
민지|en|Better: I was wondering IF go-to is commonly used. After wonder, use if or whether.
알렉스|en|You said: Because that is about to respect the mind.
민지|en|Better: Because it is a sign of respect. Fixed phrase. Also: out of respect.
알렉스|en|You said: I would recommend it to people who like ceremony day.
민지|en|Better: I would recommend it to people who would like to celebrate a special day. Use celebrate, not ceremony day.
알렉스|en|And: I don't like the stay line.
민지|en|Better: I don't like waiting in line. Or standing in line. Wait or stand goes with in line.
알렉스|en|Quick pronunciation tip before Tutor's Desk.
민지|en|The word the has two sounds. Before a consonant: thuh. Thuh drink, thuh beer, thuh bar.
알렉스|en|Before a vowel: thee. Thee Americano, thee evening. And for emphasis — the absolute best: thee. She is thee restaurant in this city.
민지|en|Thuh for normal use. Thee for vowels and for emphasis. Small detail, real impact.
알렉스|en|Tutor's Desk. Corrections from your teacher during the live lesson.
민지|en|First: Can you more drink? Natural: Can you drink more? The verb comes first, then the extra word.
알렉스|en|Can you drink more? Could you speak more slowly? Verb first, detail second. Always.
민지|en|Second: That is good for the other food. Natural: That goes well with the other food.
알렉스|en|Goes well with is a key collocation. Wine goes well with red meat. Kimchi goes well with almost everything.
민지|en|Third: your teacher highlighted go-to, hooked on, and sober this week.
알렉스|en|Go-to means your most reliable favorite. My go-to drink when I am tired is green tea. What is your go-to under stress?
민지|en|Hooked on means you love something and keep returning to it. I am hooked on this podcast. She is hooked on evening walks.
알렉스|en|Sober means not drunk, or more broadly, clear-headed and in full control.
민지|en|Own these three expressions. Recognize them AND use them this week.
알렉스|en|Vocabulary in Context now.
민지|en|Craving: a strong specific desire. I have a serious craving for tteokbokki right now.
알렉스|en|Collocations: have a craving, resist a craving, satisfy a craving, give in to your cravings.
민지|en|Spontaneously: without planning, on impulse. We decided to try that new place spontaneously. Same energy as on a whim.
알렉스|en|Ambiance: the mood and atmosphere of a place. The restaurant had a warm ambiance. Casual: the vibe was great.
민지|en|Culinary: related to food and cooking. A culinary adventure in a new city. Her culinary skills always impress.
알렉스|en|Topic Talk. Four expressions for real conversations about drinks and culture.
민지|en|One: I am a lightweight. Gets drunk easily. Two sips and I am done. Total lightweight.
알렉스|en|Two: I have developed a taste for it. Something you now enjoy that you once disliked. I have developed a taste for dry red wine.
민지|en|Three: It hits differently. Affects you in a special way. Hot soup on a cold rainy night just hits differently.
알렉스|en|Four: I am in the mood for something. I am in the mood for something light and refreshing tonight.
민지|en|Choose one and use it today. Even thinking it in English counts as practice.
알렉스|en|One more expression to borrow from this week's themes. Going out on a limb means taking a risk or making a guess you are not 100% sure about.
민지|en|I am going out on a limb here, but I think you will love this restaurant. Try it this week.
알렉스|en|It sounds dramatic — a limb of a tree — but it simply means you are taking a small risk for someone.
민지|en|Native speakers use it constantly: I will go out on a limb and say this is one of the best nights you will have.
알렉스|en|Culture Corner time. Every week, one story to make English feel more real.
민지|en|This week: a Korean word that travels to English but completely changes its meaning. 화이팅.
알렉스|en|If you shout 화이팅 to a native English speaker before a big exam, they will look confused.
민지|en|Because fighting in English means conflict — a physical or verbal fight. Not an encouraging cheer.
민지|ko|화이팅은 영어 fighting에서 왔지만, 한국에서는 완전히 다른 응원 표현이 됐어요. 원어민에게는 격려가 아니라 싸움처럼 들릴 수 있어요.
알렉스|en|What do English speakers say instead? Several natural options.
민지|en|You got this. The most common warm casual encouragement. Before any challenge at all.
알렉스|en|Go for it. Take the chance. Applied for the promotion? Go for it!
민지|en|I am rooting for you. I am on your side and I want you to succeed. Personal, sincere.
알렉스|en|Come on, you can do it! More energetic. Great for sports and competitions.
민지|en|And before a toast with English speakers, raise your glass, meet their eyes, and say: Cheers!
알렉스|en|Eye contact during a toast matters in Western culture. Looking away can seem insincere.
민지|en|Language carries culture. Knowing these gaps makes your English so much more natural.
알렉스|en|Skip 화이팅 with English speakers. Say: you got this, I am rooting for you, go for it.
민지|en|This kind of cultural knowledge is not in textbooks. But it is what makes your English feel truly connected.
알렉스|en|Every time you know the right word for the right culture, you bridge a gap. And that is what communication is really about.
