민지|en|Our headline topic this week is prepositions. Prepositions showed up as this week's grammar focus — and in the cumulative chart, prepositions are now tied for number one.
알렉스|en|Nine appearances total since May. The others tied with it? Subject-Verb Agreement and Articles. All at nine. These three are the central challenge.
민지|en|Alex, tell me — why are prepositions so hard for Korean speakers?
알렉스|en|Here's the honest answer. Korean and English handle this very differently. Korean uses particles — little endings that attach directly to nouns. 에, 에서, 으로, 에게.
민지|en|Right, the particles attach to the word. They're part of the word.
알렉스|en|English separates them into their own words — in, on, at, from, to, for, with. And the problem is: they don't line up one to one with Korean particles.
민지|ko|한국어 조사(에, 에서, 에게)와 영어 전치사(in, at, from)는 딱 1대1로 매핑되지 않아요. 어떤 전치사가 맞는지 직관적으로 느끼기 어렵죠.
알렉스|en|So Korean learners often pick the closest feeling — but English has its own specific rules for each verb and each situation.
민지|en|Let's look at a real example from this week. You said: if we graduated in university, at that time, we were throwing the cap.
알렉스|en|And the natural version: If we graduated from university, we would throw our caps.
민지|en|Two things changed. First — in university became from university. Alex, what's the difference?
알렉스|en|In university sounds like you are physically inside a building. But graduation is not about being inside — it is about leaving. Completing. Moving on.
민지|en|Like the school is the starting point, and you are moving away from it.
알렉스|en|Exactly. Think of the preposition from as an arrow pointing away. You came FROM somewhere. You graduated FROM somewhere. The school is behind you now.
민지|ko|'졸업하다'는 영어로 graduate from — 학교를 '떠나는' 분리의 느낌이에요. in은 안에 있는 느낌이라 졸업과 어울리지 않아요.
알렉스|en|Rule of thumb: graduate FROM a school, FROM a program, FROM a university. One preposition, always FROM. Make it automatic.
민지|en|And the second change — we were throwing the cap became we would throw our caps.
알렉스|en|This is the second conditional. When you have an if-clause imagining a hypothetical situation, the result uses would. If this were true — you would do that.
민지|en|So if we graduated — that's the hypothetical — we would throw our caps — that's the imagined result.
알렉스|en|Without would, the sentence sounds like a fact. With would, it sounds like you are imagining a scenario. Would creates that imaginary space.
민지|en|Now let's take another preposition example from this week. You said: the wedding place is the wedding hall.
알렉스|en|Natural version: Weddings are usually held in a wedding hall.
민지|en|What changed there?
알렉스|en|Two things. First, English uses the passive voice for events — weddings are held. Events don't just exist somewhere, they are organized and held in a location.
민지|en|Second — wedding place is a direct translation of the Korean word, but English has specific vocabulary: wedding hall, wedding venue, ceremony hall.
알렉스|en|And the preposition: held IN a wedding hall. The hall is a container — a physical space. English uses in for enclosed spaces.
민지|ko|'결혼 장소'를 직역하면 wedding place지만, 영어 원어민은 wedding hall 또는 wedding venue라고 해요.
알렉스|en|In a hall, in a stadium, in a church — the event is inside the place. The preposition in tells us the container.
민지|en|Now let's talk about verb tense — another focus this week.
알렉스|en|You said: I don't have experience with that situation, similar situation.
민지|en|And the natural version: I haven't experienced a similar situation.
알렉스|en|This is the present perfect — haven't experienced — versus the simple present — don't have. Korean often uses the simple present for experiences, but English has a special form for this.
민지|en|The present perfect connects the past to right now.
알렉스|en|Exactly. I haven't experienced that — means: from all the days of my life up until this very moment, this has not happened. It is a life-experience statement.
민지|ko|현재완료는 '지금 이 순간까지' 한 번도 없었다는 느낌이에요. 살면서 지금까지 그런 일이 없었다는 뜻이죠.
알렉스|en|Rule of thumb: for life experiences — things you have or haven't done in your life — use the present perfect. Have you ever been to Paris? I haven't experienced a similar situation.
민지|en|It is less about a specific time in the past, and more about your story up to now.
알렉스|en|If someone asks when — that is past tense. If someone asks if or ever — that is usually present perfect.
민지|en|This week's fluency score was 6.8. Down a little from the usual, but three rich topics in one week is a lot.

민지|en|Alright, Correction Clinic time! Quick and clean. Repeat after us if you can.
알렉스|en|First: You said — I don't have an appointment these days to visit the ceremony, get a wedding event.
민지|en|Natural version: I don't have any wedding invitations to attend these days.
알렉스|en|The key word is invitation. In English, going to a wedding means you were invited. You don't make an appointment — you receive an invitation. And then you attend.
민지|en|So — no wedding invitations means no weddings to go to. Clean and simple.
알렉스|en|And notice — attend, not visit. You attend a ceremony, a wedding, a conference. You visit a person or a place casually. The word choice matters.
민지|en|Attend carries a sense of being there officially, as a guest or participant. Visit is more casual.
알렉스|en|Next: You said — My achievement of one thing is to stay my weight for 30 years.
민지|en|Natural version: One achievement I'm proud of is maintaining my weight for 20 years.
알렉스|en|Notice the gerund — maintaining — it acts like a noun. And proud of gives it an emotional, personal feel. Much stronger.
민지|en|You feel the pride in that sentence. That is the difference.
알렉스|en|Next: You said — Your husband is so urgent.
민지|en|Natural version: Your husband was so eager — or impatient.
알렉스|en|This is a false friend situation. Urgent in English means something requires immediate attention — urgent email, urgent situation. It is about a task or problem, not a person's personality.
민지|en|So it would be strange to call a person urgent.
알렉스|en|Right. For people who act quickly or can't wait, English says eager — enthusiastic and ready — or impatient — not able to wait calmly.
민지|en|Eager sounds positive. Impatient sounds like they can't slow down. Two different shades of the same energy.
알렉스|en|Great instinct to notice the emotion, though. Now it's just about finding the English word that matches.
민지|en|Now let's move to Vocabulary in Context. Not just the definition — how the word lives in real sentences.
알렉스|en|First: officiant. An officiant is the person who leads and conducts a formal ceremony — a wedding, a memorial service, a naming ceremony.
민지|en|They make the ceremony official — which is exactly where the word comes from!
알렉스|en|Great collocation: serve as the officiant, the officiant welcomed the guests, the couple chose a close friend as their officiant.
민지|en|Next: diploma. A diploma is the official document you receive when you complete a degree or a formal program.
알렉스|en|Different from a certificate. A certificate is for completing a course or passing a test. A diploma is for finishing a degree. She framed her diploma and hung it proudly.
민지|en|And valedictorian — the student with the highest grades who gives the graduation speech.
알렉스|en|He worked hard for four years and became valedictorian of his class. You see this word in school dramas and movies all the time.
민지|en|Now — Topic Talk! Five expressions around weddings and milestone events.
알렉스|en|Number one: tie the knot. To tie the knot means to get married. They tied the knot in a small ceremony by the beach.
민지|en|The image is two pieces of rope tied together. Two lives joined as one. It's a very warm, informal expression.
알렉스|en|Number two: walk down the aisle. The aisle is the center path in a ceremony hall. She walked down the aisle to the sound of a live violin.
민지|en|Number three: give a toast. At celebrations, you raise your glass and speak kind words to honor someone. The best man gave a heartfelt toast at the reception.
알렉스|en|Number four: RSVP. From French — Répondez s'il vous plaît — it means please respond. When you get a wedding invitation, it usually says RSVP by a date. You reply to say if you are coming.
민지|en|If you don't RSVP, the host doesn't know how many people are coming. It is a simple but important social rule.
알렉스|en|Number five: milestone. A milestone is a significant moment in life — a graduation, a wedding, a promotion. Getting your first job is a real milestone.
민지|en|Milestone comes from an actual stone marker on a road that showed how many miles you had traveled. Now it's used for any major life moment.

민지|en|A thunder machine! In 1700! That must have been incredible for audiences at the time.
알렉스|en|His invention was genuinely impressive. The problem was — his play was terrible. It flopped completely. The theater closed it within days.
민지|en|Oh no! He worked so hard and the play failed.
알렉스|en|And then the theater replaced his play with Shakespeare's Macbeth. A much more popular choice.
민지|en|That's fair, I suppose. But then what?
알렉스|en|But — they kept using his thunder machine! For someone else's successful play!
민지|en|That is so unfair!
알렉스|en|Dennis was furious. He reportedly jumped up in the audience during a performance and shouted: They will not let my play run, and yet they steal my thunder!
민지|en|He invented the phrase on the spot! In the moment of fury!
알렉스|en|Yes! And the phrase lived on — while his play was completely forgotten. The ultimate irony.
민지|ko|"steal one's thunder"는 1700년대 극작가 존 데니스가 자신이 발명한 천둥 효과기를 극장이 무단으로 써버리자 "내 천둥을 훔쳐갔다!"고 외친 데서 나왔어요. 지금은 남의 공이나 주목을 가로챈다는 뜻으로 씁니다.
알렉스|en|Today, to steal someone's thunder means to take attention or credit that should belong to someone else. I was about to announce my big news, but my coworker announced their promotion first and completely stole my thunder.
