민지|en|Hello and welcome back to your Weekly English Radio.
민지|en|I'm Minji, your warm host.
알렉스|en|And I'm Alex, your English coach.
알렉스|en|This is 2026-W41, the week of October 5th to 9th.
민지|en|Four lessons this week. Consistent, solid effort.
알렉스|en|Topics covered: daily routines, health habits, shopping preferences, buyer's remorse, and a tech troubleshooting session before class.
민지|en|A full week. Today we go deep.
알렉스|en|Not just listing mistakes. We want to understand why they happen and how to shift your thinking next time.
민지|en|Awareness first, perfection later. That's our philosophy here. Let's go.
알렉스|en|Our headline pattern, the all-time record holder in our log: Articles. A, an, and the. Thirteen times since the cutoff.
민지|en|Thirteen! Alex, why does this one keep coming back every single week?
알렉스|en|The honest answer: Korean has no articles. Your language handles context without them beautifully.
알렉스|en|So to your brain, a and the feel like optional decoration. Not important grammar.
민지|ko|한국어에는 a, the가 없어요. 없어도 뜻이 통하니까 뇌가 '그냥 장식'이라고 학습한 거예요. 그런데 영어에서 관사는 정보 신호예요.
알렉스|en|Right. Articles are signals. They tell your listener something important.
민지|en|What do they tell the listener?
알렉스|en|A or an says: here comes new information. The listener doesn't know about this yet.
알렉스|en|The says: we both already know which one I mean.
민지|en|And no article at all?
알렉스|en|No article means you're talking about a whole category. Not one thing. The general concept.
민지|ko|정리: a는 새 정보, the는 서로 아는 그것, 무관사는 종류 전체예요. 이 세 패턴만 알면 관사의 70퍼센트가 해결돼요.
알렉스|en|Your rule of thumb: ask yourself, does my listener already know which one I mean?
알렉스|en|Yes? Use the. No? Use a or an. General category? Use nothing.
민지|en|Let's see it in action. You said: I love the classic and upscale store because it gives comfort for me.
알렉스|en|Better: I love classic and upscale stores because they give me comfort.
민지|en|Two changes at once. Classic stores with no article and plural form.
알렉스|en|Because you mean that type of store in general, not one specific store. So zero article, and we use the plural.
민지|en|And it gives comfort for me became they give me comfort.
알렉스|en|Stores is plural, so the pronoun becomes they and the verb becomes give.
알렉스|en|And comfort for me sounds awkward. We say give me comfort or bring me comfort.
민지|en|One sentence, three lessons. Articles, pronoun agreement, and natural word order. Rich!
알렉스|en|Now let's talk about Gerunds. Monday's grammar focus. A gerund is a verb with ing that works like a noun.
민지|en|Like swimming is my hobby or I enjoy cooking.
알렉스|en|In Korean, verbs can act as objects without changing form. But in English, you usually need the ing ending.
민지|ko|한국어는 '수영 좋아해'처럼 동사를 그대로 쓰지만, 영어는 I love swimming처럼 -ing가 필요해요.
알렉스|en|A real example: I don't want to do because it's so tired. Better: I don't want to because it's so tiring.
민지|en|Tiring versus tired. What's the difference?
알렉스|en|Tired describes how a person feels. Tiring describes how the activity makes you feel.
알렉스|en|The exercise is tiring. After the exercise, you feel tired. Two different adjectives.
민지|en|Tired person. Tiring activity. Quick and very useful.
알렉스|en|And our second big pattern: Subject-Verb Agreement. Eleven times in the record.
민지|en|The classic example: I shop online most of the time. It save times.
알렉스|en|Better: It saves time. Two fixes in four short words.
민지|en|Fix one: saves not save.
알렉스|en|When the subject is he, she, or it, the simple present verb always gets an s.
민지|ko|주어가 he, she, it이면 동사에 -s. 한국어는 주어가 달라도 동사가 같아서 이 -s가 자꾸 빠져요.
알렉스|en|Fix two: time not times. Time here is uncountable. A resource, not individual pieces you can count.
민지|en|It saves time, money, energy. Not times, moneys, or energies.
알렉스|en|Rule of thumb: he, she, it? Check the verb. Does it end in s? Add it if not.
민지|en|Correction Clinic time. Quick fire. Repeat after us if you can.
알렉스|en|You said: I'm a little afraid of something.
민지|en|More natural: I'm a little worried about all the work waiting for me.
알렉스|en|Worried about something specific sounds more grounded and natural.
민지|en|You said: I do everything.
알렉스|en|Better: I'll handle everything. Handle sounds more confident and natural.
민지|en|You said: I moved to another area because I have to work tomorrow.
알렉스|en|Better: I'm staying in another area because I have to work tomorrow.
민지|en|You're already there. The action is ongoing. I'm staying describes the current state.
알렉스|en|You said: I think that's meaning is buyer regret.
민지|en|Better: I think its meaning is buyer's remorse.
알렉스|en|That's is that is. Here you need the possessive its, no apostrophe. And the phrase is buyer's remorse.
민지|en|You said: Before the next go on, I have a question about the remorse.
알렉스|en|Better: Before we move on, I have a question about remorse.
민지|en|The phrase is move on, not go on. And remorse without the because you mean the general concept.
알렉스|en|You said: I like shopping in store because I can try thing on.
민지|en|Better: I like shopping in-store because I can try things on.
알렉스|en|In-store with a hyphen is a compound adjective. And things plural because you try multiple items.
민지|en|You said: I need to figure out a little more. I wait for the sale periods.
알렉스|en|Better: I need to do a bit more research. I'm waiting for this product to go on sale.
민지|en|Figure out means to understand or solve a puzzle. Here you mean research, not solving.
알렉스|en|And the natural expression is go on sale, not sale periods.
민지|en|You said: do you hear my voice?
알렉스|en|Better: Can you hear me? Short, direct, and natural when the audio cuts out.
민지|en|Tutor's Desk. Six corrections from your live class on October 5th.
알렉스|en|These came straight from real conversation. Premium signals.
민지|en|You said: Afternoon I'll go back my office.
알렉스|en|Better: In the afternoon I'll go back to my office.
민지|en|Time expressions like in the afternoon need a preposition. And go back needs to before the destination.
알렉스|ko|go back to 다음에 장소가 필수예요. to가 빠지면 원어민은 즉시 어색함을 느껴요.
민지|en|You said: Usually I work the every Tuesday. Better: Usually I work out every Tuesday.
알렉스|en|Work out is the verb for exercise. The before every is wrong. Every never needs an article.
민지|en|You said: I realize that want to go back my home after work.
알렉스|en|Better: I realize that I want to go back home after work.
민지|en|The subject I was dropped inside the that-clause. English requires a subject in every clause.
알렉스|en|You said: That is both important. Better: Those are both important.
민지|en|Two things, so we need those and are. Subject-verb agreement again!
알렉스|en|You said: We want to every things eat but that is control.
민지|en|Better: We want to eat everything but that needs to be controlled.
알렉스|en|Want to eat, not separated. Everything as one word. And is control becomes needs to be controlled.
민지|en|Great message inside a tough sentence. Just needed the English shape around it.
알렉스|en|Vocabulary in Context. Words come alive in real sentences.
민지|en|Word one: apprehensive. Nervous or uneasy about something coming. I feel a bit apprehensive before each lesson, but once I start, it's fine.
알렉스|en|Word two: persevere. To keep going even when things are hard. Even on tired days, I persevere and show up.
민지|en|Word three: remorse. Deep regret, stronger than just sorry. I felt buyer's remorse after buying shoes I never wore.
알렉스|en|Word four: upscale. High quality, fancy, expensive. An upscale restaurant, an upscale neighborhood.
민지|en|Word five: troubleshoot. To find and fix a problem step by step. I spent ten minutes troubleshooting my audio. Sound familiar?
알렉스|en|Topic Talk. Shopping was a big theme this week. Here are four bonus phrases.
민지|en|First: to window shop. To browse without buying. I just went to window shop, not to spend anything.
알렉스|en|Second: to go on sale. The price drops. I'm waiting for the Mac Mini to go on sale.
민지|en|Third: to splurge. To spend more than usual as a treat. I splurged on a nice dinner last Friday.
알렉스|en|Fourth: a bargain. Something bought for much less than its real value. I found a great bargain at the second-hand store.
민지|en|Did You Know time! One of my favorite segments.
알렉스|en|This week you talked about shopping malls. But where does the word mall actually come from?
민지|en|Oh, etymology! I love this. Tell me.
알렉스|en|In 17th century England, there was a popular game called pall-mall.
알렉스|en|Think of it like early croquet. Players hit a ball through a hoop along a long, tree-lined alley.
민지|en|So The Mall in London, the walkway near Buckingham Palace, was the actual alley for this game?
알렉스|en|Exactly. The game faded away, but the beautiful long walkway stayed.
알렉스|en|Over time, any long enclosed walkway lined with shops borrowed the same name. That's why we say shopping mall.
민지|ko|쇼핑몰의 mall은 17세기 영국의 공 게임 팰-팰을 하던 긴 골목길에서 왔어요. 게임은 사라졌지만 이름은 400년을 살아남았죠!
알렉스|en|Every word in English carries a hidden story. That's what makes this language so fascinating.

알렉스|en|Mission two: when the subject is it, he, or she, check the verb. Does it have an s?
알렉스|en|Saves, works, gives, runs. One small habit that makes a big difference.
