민지|en|Hello everyone, and welcome back to 당근영어 Radio! I am Minji, your host.
알렉스|en|And I am Alex, your English coach. This is Week 34, and you completed five lessons this week!
민지|en|Five lessons on phones, travel, email writing, phone repair, and birthday celebrations. A really full week.
알렉스|en|And today, we are not just going to list your mistakes. We are going to understand them.
민지|en|Why do we say the wrong thing? And how do English speakers think differently?
알렉스|en|That is the real learning. Let us start with the two biggest patterns this week.
민지|en|And actually, they have been the top weaknesses for weeks now. Articles and Prepositions.
알렉스|en|Both are at eight or nine occurrences in the cumulative count. They keep coming back.
민지|en|So let us really dig in this week. Alex, can you start with Articles?
알렉스|en|Sure! This week our learner said: I am more older generation, so I like to phone call.
민지|en|I hear two problems there. One is double comparative. We say older, not more older.
알렉스|en|Right. But the article problem is also there. The natural version is: I am from AN older generation.
민지|en|An! Because older starts with a vowel sound.
알렉스|en|Exactly. An older generation, an easy question, an interesting idea. A before consonant sounds, an before vowel sounds.
민지|ko|한국어에는 관사가 없잖아요. '나는 나이 든 세대야'라고 하면 그냥 끝이에요. 영어는 항상 a 또는 an을 써야 하는데, 한국어 사고 습관으로는 이 단계가 자동으로 안 돼요.
알렉스|en|That is exactly it. Korean has no articles. So your brain naturally skips that step.
민지|en|What is a good rule of thumb for articles?
알렉스|en|Here is one: is this the first time you mention it? Use a or an. Does the listener already know which one? Use the.
민지|en|So: I saw A dog on the street. Then: THE dog was very big. First time, then known.
알렉스|en|Perfect! And for uncountable nouns? No article. I love music. Not I love a music.
민지|en|Speaking of countable and uncountable, we had another pattern this week.
알렉스|en|Yes. The learner said: many young generations have a relationship, use Instagram.
민지|en|And the correction is: many young PEOPLE connect with each other using Instagram.
알렉스|en|Why people, not generations?
민지|en|Because generation means a whole age group, like everyone born in the 1980s or 1990s.
알렉스|en|Right. If you say many young generations, it sounds like entire historical generations are all on Instagram at once.
민지|ko|'젊은 세대들'은 한국어에서 자연스럽지만, 영어에서는 'young people' 또는 'younger generations'처럼 말해야 더 자연스러워요. '세대'를 복수로 쓰면 범위가 너무 넓어집니다.
알렉스|en|So for groups of individuals, say young people, people in their twenties, younger generations, and so on.
민지|en|Great. Now let us move to prepositions. These small words cause big trouble.
알렉스|en|And the key idea is this: prepositions travel with their partner words. You cannot learn them alone.
민지|en|This week we heard: many people went TO summer vacation.
알렉스|en|And the correction is: many people went ON summer vacation. Go ON vacation, not TO vacation.
민지|en|And we also heard: some people search SOME information used in YouTube.
알렉스|en|Two fixes here. First: search FOR information. Second: ON YouTube, not IN YouTube.
민지|ko|한국어는 '~을 검색하다'고 하면 목적어가 바로 오잖아요. 영어는 search FOR처럼 전치사가 반드시 필요해요. 그리고 온라인 플랫폼은 in이 아니라 on을 써요. on Instagram, on YouTube, on the internet.
알렉스|en|Exactly. We are ON Instagram, ON YouTube, ON the internet. Not in them.
민지|en|Another example: it is popular TO our population.
알렉스|en|Correction: popular AMONG our population. Among means within a group of people.
민지|en|And the rule of thumb for prepositions?
알렉스|en|Never study a preposition alone. Study it with its partner. Not just popular, but popular AMONG. Not just search, but search FOR. Not just go, but go ON vacation.
민지|en|Learn the chunk, not the word. Great advice. Now, Correction Clinic!
알렉스|en|Correction Clinic! Quick and clean. Repeat after us if you can.
민지|en|You said: My phone alarm worked, is it right?
알렉스|en|Better: My phone alarm went off. Went off is the phrasal verb for an alarm ringing.
민지|en|The alarm went off at seven. The fire alarm went off in the building.
알렉스|en|You said: I put in charging port in my mobile phone.
민지|en|Better: I plug my phone INTO the charging port. Plug into, not put in.
알렉스|en|You said: It is cutting to my head.
민지|en|Better: It makes me think. Or: it is thought-provoking. Cutting to my head sounds like a physical injury!
알렉스|en|You said: Tomorrow we will travel.
민지|en|Better: Tomorrow we are traveling. When the plan is confirmed, use present continuous for the future.
알렉스|en|We are traveling tomorrow. We are meeting on Friday. We are flying to Singapore next week.
민지|en|You said: Usually we will send the message email and sometimes he replies my email.
알렉스|en|Better: Usually we send emails, and sometimes we get replies. For habits and routines, use simple present, not will.
민지|en|I usually wake up at seven. She usually drives to work. Simple present for what you always do.
알렉스|en|You said: I am taking twice per day, two classes per day.
민지|en|Better: I am taking two classes A DAY. A day means per day. Twice a day, three times a week, once a month.
알렉스|en|You said: But it is possible because some sentences are misprinted, but almost 99% printed is well.
민지|en|Better: Some sentences are misprinted, but about 99 percent of the printout is fine. Not printed is well, but is fine.
알렉스|en|Good clinic! Now let us go to Tutor's Desk.
민지|en|Tutor's Desk is where we look at corrections from your real classroom. This week, thirty corrections!
알렉스|en|Thirty! That is a lot of great, detailed feedback from your teacher.
민지|en|Let us start with the pronunciation corrections. These are special.
알렉스|en|Yes. Your teacher wrote: you said doom SCREAMING before bed.
민지|en|Screaming! The word is doom SCROLLING. D-O-O-M S-C-R-O-L-L-I-N-G.
알렉스|en|Doom scrolling means endlessly scrolling through bad news on your phone, especially late at night.
민지|ko|'둠스크롤링'은 밤에 나쁜 뉴스를 계속 스크롤하는 행동이에요. 멈추고 싶은데 계속하게 되죠. 많이 하시나요?
알렉스|en|Another pronunciation fix: the learner said PONE ALUM. The teacher corrected it to FONE AH-LARM. Phone alarm.
민지|en|Let us say it slowly together. Phone. Alarm. Phone alarm.
알렉스|en|And one more: ALOT. That is not a word! The correct word is ALERT. AH-LURT.
민지|en|I received a usage ALERT after watching videos on 4G. Not a lot, but alert.
알렉스|en|Now let us look at grammar from Tutor's Desk. The teacher caught: Every place HAVE a pros and cons.
민지|en|Correction: Every place HAS pros and cons. Every always takes a singular verb.
알렉스|en|Every student IS here. Every place HAS challenges. Every person KNOWS this rule now!
민지|en|The teacher also caught: Last night I WILL install some application.
알렉스|en|If it happened last night, use past tense. Last night I INSTALLED some applications.
민지|en|Will is for the future. Last night is the past. Simple.
알렉스|en|And: Hard rest always good way to fix phone.
민지|en|Three things to fix! First, it is a hard RESET, not rest. Second, we need an article: A hard reset. Third, the verb is missing: A hard reset IS always a good way to fix a phone.
알렉스|en|The teacher is catching layers. Article, vocabulary, and missing verb all in one sentence.
민지|en|And one more from Tutor: I am just BLOCK this person.
알렉스|en|Correction: I will just BLOCK this person. Or: I am going to block this person. We need an auxiliary verb.
민지|en|For quick decisions in English, say: I will do it, I am going to do it, or I am doing it right now.
알렉스|en|Perfect. Now, Vocabulary in Context and Topic Talk!
민지|en|Let us power up the words from this week with real sentences and collocations.
알렉스|en|First: pocket dial. Have you ever done this?
민지|en|Yes! Pocket dialing is when your phone accidentally calls someone while it is in your pocket.
알렉스|en|I accidentally pocket-dialed my boss during lunch. Very embarrassing.
민지|en|In informal English, people also say butt-dial because you sit on your phone. Same meaning.
알렉스|en|Next: break up. Not like ending a relationship! In phone calls, the connection is breaking up.
민지|en|Sorry, you are breaking up. I cannot hear you clearly. The call kept breaking up.
알렉스|en|And: doom scrolling. Endlessly scrolling bad news late at night. I doom-scrolled for two hours last night.
민지|en|For Topic Talk this week, three extra expressions for digital life and communication!
알렉스|en|Expression one: I am running low on data. This means your mobile data is almost finished.
민지|en|I am running low on battery too! Running low on something means it is almost empty.
알렉스|en|Expression two: my flight got delayed. Not my flight was late. The natural phrase is: got delayed.
민지|en|The meeting got delayed. The package got delayed. The project got delayed. Very common in work English.
알렉스|en|Expression three: let me follow up with you. This means I will contact you again about this topic later.
민지|en|I will follow up with you after the meeting. Can you follow up with the client by Friday?
알렉스|en|Very useful for emails and business conversations.
민지|en|Great! Now it is time for my favorite part. Culture Corner!
알렉스|en|What is this week's story?
민지|en|This week we are talking about the origin of the word deadline.
알렉스|en|Deadline! We use this word every day. Where does it come from?
민지|en|It actually comes from American Civil War prisons in the 1860s.
알렉스|en|What? That sounds serious.
민지|en|In prisoner camps, guards drew a line on the ground around the prison area.
알렉스|en|And if a prisoner crossed that line?
민지|en|The guards would shoot them immediately. That line was called the dead line. Cross it and you die.
알렉스|ko|원래 'deadline'은 정말로 죽음의 선이었어요. 그 선을 넘으면 총을 맞았거든요. 그래서 지금도 마감을 deadline이라고 하면 절대 넘으면 안 되는 느낌이 드는 거예요.
알렉스|en|So next time your boss says do not miss the deadline, they are being more correct than they know!
민지|en|Language carries history inside it. That is what makes English so fascinating.
알렉스|en|And the word spread. Now we have deadlines at work, school, and everywhere else.
민지|en|If only crossing office deadlines was as dramatic as the original meaning!
알렉스|en|Ha! Right. Okay, let us wrap up with the Recap and your Mission for next week.
민지|en|This week in numbers: five lessons, fluency average 7.2 this week, and 7.1 cumulative. Very stable!
알렉스|en|Sixty-one lessons since the cutoff. That is real commitment. You showed up every single day this week.
민지|en|Cumulative top weaknesses are Subject-Verb Agreement and Articles, both at nine occurrences each.
알렉스|en|Here is the thing: these are not random. They are patterns. And patterns can be fixed.
민지|en|Your mission for next week has two parts. Part one: before every sentence, check your subject.
알렉스|en|Is the subject singular or plural? Then match your verb. He has, not he have. Every place has, not have.
민지|en|Part two: learn your prepositions as chunks. Go ON vacation. Search FOR information. Plug INTO the charger. Popular AMONG people.
알렉스|en|Do not study the preposition alone. Always study it with the verb or adjective it belongs to.
민지|en|And remember: awareness first, perfection later. Just noticing the pattern is already progress.
알렉스|en|You are doing great. Five days, five lessons, thirty corrections caught and reviewed.
