민지|en|Hello everyone, and welcome back to 당근영어 Radio!
민지|en|I am Minji, your host, and as always, Alex is here with me.
알렉스|en|Hey everyone! Great to be with you again this week.
민지|en|This week you completed five lessons — phone calls, digital habits, travel plans, phone repair, and birthday celebrations.
알렉스|en|Five topics, five days. That is impressive commitment!
민지|en|And today we are not just listing mistakes. We want to understand WHY they happen.
알렉스|en|Because when you understand the pattern, you can change it for good.
민지|en|Our biggest focus today: Subject-Verb Agreement. It has been at the top of the cumulative weakness list for weeks.
알렉스|en|Nine occurrences in the cumulative count. It keeps coming back. So let us really dig into it today.
민지|en|Let us go!
알렉스|en|Here is a real sentence from this week: Every place have a pros and cons.
민지|en|I hear this kind of sentence often. What is wrong there, Alex?
알렉스|en|The key word is "every." In English, "every" always goes with a singular verb.
알렉스|en|So the correct sentence is: Every place HAS its pros and cons.
민지|en|Every place HAS. Not have. But why do so many learners make this mistake?
알렉스|ko|한국어에서는 주어가 단수인지 복수인지에 따라 동사가 바뀌지 않아요. "모든 곳은 장단점이 있다" — 동사는 그냥 "있다"예요. 영어는 반드시 has로 바꿔야 하는데 이 단계가 자동화되지 않은 거죠.
알렉스|en|In Korean, the verb does not change based on singular or plural subjects. So your brain naturally skips that step.
민지|en|What is a good rule of thumb?
알렉스|en|Simple: "every" is always singular. Every person IS here. Every idea HAS its time. Every mistake MAKES you better.
민지|en|Try it with me. Every lesson...
알렉스|en|Every lesson HELPS. Every lesson IS worth it. Never "every lesson help."
민지|en|Good! Now here is another sentence from this week: My son and me promise together.
알렉스|en|Two problems here. First, the subject form is "I," not "me." I is a subject. Me is an object.
민지|en|Easy trick: remove the other person and test it. Would you say "Me promise"? No! You say "I promise."
알렉스|en|Right. So the natural version: My son and I made a promise to each other.
민지|en|My son and I. Not me. Got it!
알렉스|en|And one more from this week: When a coding, my AI goes to auto correction.
알렉스|en|The correct version: When I am coding, my AI automatically corrects my code.
민지|en|The subject I was missing! Always ask: who is doing the action? That is your subject.
알렉스|en|Now let us talk about Articles — the other number one weakness, also at nine occurrences cumulative.
민지|en|This week we heard: I have some suggestion about you.
알렉스|en|Suggestion is a countable noun. Singular. So it needs an article. The correct version: I have A suggestion.
알렉스|ko|한국어에는 관사가 없잖아요. "제안이 있어요"라고 하면 끝이에요. 영어는 셀 수 있는 단수 명사 앞에 무조건 a 또는 the를 붙여야 해요. 한국어 뇌에는 이 단계가 없으니까 계속 빠지는 거예요.
알렉스|en|Rule: if it is countable and singular, give it an article. A suggestion, a question, a problem, a phone.
민지|en|And uncountable nouns do not need an article. I love music. Not I love a music.
알렉스|en|I need water. Not I need a water. Unless you mean one glass — then a water is short for a glass of water.
민지|en|Now, prepositions — the third biggest weakness, at eight occurrences cumulative.
알렉스|en|Classic from this week: Many people went TO summer vacation.
민지|en|Is that wrong? We go TO places, right?
알렉스|en|Yes, but vacation is not a place. It is a state or activity. So we say: went ON summer vacation.
알렉스|ko|한국어로는 "여름휴가를 갔다"가 자연스럽죠. 영어는 vacation이 장소가 아니라 상태이기 때문에 go on vacation을 써요. on vacation, on a trip, on a break — 이 묶음 표현을 통째로 외워두세요.
민지|en|Go ON vacation, go ON a trip, go ON a business trip. The word "on" shows a state or activity.
알렉스|en|Another one from this week: Some people search some information used in YouTube. Two preposition problems!
민지|en|What are they?
알렉스|en|First: you search FOR information. Not just search information. Second: ON YouTube, not IN YouTube.
민지|en|We are ON platforms. On YouTube, on Instagram, on the internet. Not in them.
알렉스|en|Rule of thumb: never study a preposition alone. Learn the whole chunk. Search FOR. Go ON vacation. Popular AMONG. Plug INTO.
민지|en|Time for Correction Clinic! Let us go through more corrections quickly. Repeat after me if you can!
알렉스|en|You said: My phone alarm worked. Better: My phone alarm went OFF.
민지|en|Go off means a sound suddenly starts. The alarm went off at six. The fire alarm went off.
알렉스|en|You said: I put in charging port in my mobile phone. Better: I plug my phone INTO the charging port.
민지|en|Plug INTO. Not put in. Plug your phone into the wall. Plug your headphones into your laptop.
알렉스|en|You said: Tomorrow we will travel. Better: Tomorrow we are traveling.
민지|en|When the plan is already arranged, use present continuous for the future. We are meeting on Friday. We are flying to China next week.
알렉스|en|You said: I am taking twice per day, two classes per day. Better: I am taking two classes A day.
민지|en|A day means per day. Twice a day. Three times a week. Once a month. Very natural in spoken English!
알렉스|en|You said: So in Korea it is popular to our population. Better: popular AMONG our population in Korea.
민지|en|Among means within a group. Popular among young people. Common among students.
알렉스|en|You said: I am more older generation. Better: I am FROM an older generation.
민지|en|Two fixes: from shows which group you belong to, and older is the correct comparative — not more older.
알렉스|en|We say older, younger, taller — never more older or more younger. The -er ending is already comparative!
민지|en|Now let us go to Tutor's Desk — corrections straight from your real live classroom!
알렉스|en|This week your tutor gave fifty-one corrections. That is very detailed, high-quality feedback.
민지|en|First, pronunciation corrections. The tutor caught something funny this week.
알렉스|en|You said: doom SCREAMING before bed. The word is doom SCROLLING. S-C-R-O-L-L-I-N-G.
민지|en|Doom scrolling is endlessly scrolling through bad news on your phone at night. We have all done this!
알렉스|ko|둠스크롤링은 2020년 코로나 시기에 유행한 신조어예요. 불안한 뉴스를 멈추지 못하고 계속 스크롤하는 그 상태를 가리켜요.
알렉스|en|Next pronunciation: you said PONE ALUM. The teacher corrected it: FONE AH-LARM. Phone alarm.
민지|en|Let us say it slowly. Phone. F-OWN. And alarm. AH-LARM. Phone alarm!
알렉스|en|The tutor also corrected: I don't REMIND some good service. The correct word is REMEMBER.
민지|en|Remind and remember are different. Remember is in your own mind. Remind is something you do for another person.
알렉스|en|I remember my teacher's name. But: Can you remind ME about the meeting? You remind someone else.
민지|en|The tutor also caught: The temperature is the 30 degree in daytime. How many things to fix?
알렉스|en|Three! No "the" before 30. Degrees is plural. And "during the day" sounds more natural than "in daytime."
민지|en|Correct version: The temperature is around 30 degrees during the day. Clean and natural.
알렉스|en|And the tutor caught: Last night I will install some application.
민지|en|Last night is in the past! The verb must be past tense.
알렉스|en|Correct: Last night I installed some apps. Will is for the future. Installed is for the past.
민지|en|Time matters in English — always match your verb to when it happened.
알렉스|en|Now, Vocabulary in Context! Let us make this week's words stick with real collocations.
민지|en|First word: pocket dial. This means your phone accidentally calls someone while it is in your pocket.
알렉스|en|I accidentally pocket-dialed my boss during lunch. Very embarrassing!
민지|en|People also say butt dial — same meaning, but when your phone dials someone because you sat on it.
알렉스|en|Next: break up. In phone calls, breaking up means the audio is choppy and unclear.
민지|en|Sorry, you are breaking up — I cannot hear you! Very different from breaking up in a relationship!
알렉스|en|Topic Talk: three bonus expressions for digital and travel life.
민지|en|One: my flight got delayed. Much more natural than my flight was late.
알렉스|en|The meeting got postponed. The parcel got delayed. The event got canceled. Got plus past participle!
민지|en|Two: I am running low on data. Running low means almost finished.
알렉스|en|Running low on battery. Running low on time. Running low on patience! Very useful for many situations.
민지|en|Three: Can you get back to me on that? This means please reply to me later about this topic.
알렉스|en|I will get back to you by Friday. Thanks for your patience — I will get back to you soon.
민지|en|Professional, natural, and very common in work emails and business conversations.
알렉스|en|Time for Culture Corner! This week's story comes from the birthday and celebration lesson.
민지|en|Yes! We talked about parties, milestone events, and big celebrations. That made me think of theater.
알렉스|en|Theater? Why theater?
민지|en|Because of a famous theater expression: break a leg!
알렉스|en|Of course. Actors say it to each other before a performance instead of saying good luck.
민지|en|But WHY would you tell someone to break their leg before going on stage?
알렉스|en|One popular theory comes from the theater itself. The curtains hanging on the sides of the stage are called legs.
민지|en|Really? The curtains are called legs?
알렉스|en|Yes! So to break a leg means to push through those curtains — meaning to actually step out on stage and perform!
알렉스|ko|"break a leg"는 무대 양쪽 커튼을 "leg"라고 부른 데서 왔다는 설이 있어요. 즉 "무대에 서라!" 라는 뜻이었던 거죠. 무대에 서는 것 자체가 이미 성공이니까요.
알렉스|en|Another theory: actors believed saying good luck was actually bad luck. So they said the opposite to cancel it!
민지|en|Theater superstitions are very strong! Did you know actors also never say the title of a famous Shakespeare play inside a theater?
알렉스|en|That is right. They call it the Scottish play instead. Saying the real title is considered very bad luck.
민지|en|So next time someone has a big presentation or a job interview — tell them: break a leg!
알렉스|en|Just explain it first so they do not think you are being unkind!
