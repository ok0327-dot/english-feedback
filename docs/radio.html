민지|en|Hello everyone, and welcome back to your Weekly English Radio. I'm Minji, your host.
알렉스|en|And I'm Alex, your English coach. This is week 39 of 2026, September 21 to 25.
민지|en|Two lessons this week on two great topics: autumn traditions and Korean drinking culture.
알렉스|en|Very real, very everyday topics. Exactly the kind of English you need for real conversations.
민지|en|And today, we are not just reading out your corrections. We are going to understand WHY they happen.
알렉스|en|Because when you understand why, the correction sticks forever. Let's start with the big story this week.
민지|en|Articles. A, an, and the. The number one cumulative weakness. Twelve times since May.
알렉스|en|Twelve times. So today, we are giving articles a full, deep treatment. No skipping.
민지|en|Alex, let's start from the very beginning. Why does this keep coming back?
알렉스|en|One word: Korean. Korean has no articles at all. Zero. Not one.
민지|ko|한국어에는 관사가 전혀 없어요. '사과' 하나가 a apple, the apple, apples 를 모두 표현하죠. 그래서 a 와 the 가 영어에서 있어도 없어도 되는 것처럼 느껴지는 거예요.
알렉스|en|So articles feel optional. Like decoration. But they are not optional. They carry real meaning.
민지|en|They carry real meaning. What kind of meaning, Alex?
알렉스|en|A or an says: I am introducing this for the first time. New. The says: we both already know this one. Shared.
민지|en|I love this image. Think of a and an as turning on a new spotlight. Something the listener has not seen yet.
알렉스|en|And the is pointing to a spotlight that is already on. Already in our shared view.
민지|en|New spotlight: a. Already shining: the. That is the whole rule.
알렉스|en|Now let's hear it in your own sentences from this week.
민지|en|You said: Maybe how about your country's beauty? The natural version: What about the autumn in your country?
알렉스|en|Why the autumn, and not just autumn? Because there is only one autumn per year. We both already know which one. Shared knowledge means the.
민지|en|Seasons in context almost always take the. The spring was warm. The summer was too short. Enjoy the autumn.
알렉스|en|And unique things in our shared world also take the. The sun, the moon, the sky, the internet, the ocean.
민지|en|Quick tip: if you can say there is only one of it in the world we share, use the.
알렉스|en|Let's make this practical. Mini quiz time. I will describe a noun. You decide: a or the?
민지|en|Ready? You are texting your friend. You just passed by a restaurant on your walk. Your friend has never heard of it.
알렉스|en|New or shared?
민지|en|New! First mention. So: I passed by a restaurant.
알렉스|en|Good. Next morning, you say to the same friend: I went back to the same restaurant we talked about.
민지|en|Now your friend knows exactly which one. Shared. So: I went back to the restaurant.
알렉스|en|Same restaurant. Different articles. Different moments. A for first mention, the after that.
민지|en|You can play this game in every conversation today. Every noun: new or known? It builds the habit fast.
알렉스|en|Now let's look at the second big pattern this week. It's about time and duration.
민지|en|You said: my bag is old, almost three years more past. The natural version: it's been over three years.
알렉스|en|In Korean, you say 3년이 지났다. Past tense. The time passed. Finished.
민지|ko|한국어에서는 '3년이 지났다'고 과거형으로 말하죠. 하지만 영어에서 그 기간이 지금까지 이어지고 있을 때는 현재완료 it's been 을 써야 합니다.
알렉스|en|In English, the time started in the past and still continues until this moment. Present perfect: it's been.
민지|en|It's been over three years. I've been learning English for two years. She's been my best friend since school.
알렉스|en|Rule of thumb: if the clock started in the past but is still ticking right now, use it's been or I've been.
민지|en|One more thing about articles before the clinic. There is a third case that trips people up.
알렉스|en|When you mean something in general, for all cases, use no article. Dogs are loyal. Coffee is popular. Music helps you focus.
민지|en|So three choices: a specific new thing gets a or an. A specific known thing gets the. A general category gets nothing.
알렉스|en|Most Korean speakers put the in front of everything. The water is important. But if you mean water in general, just say water is important.
민지|en|This week you talked about drinks. So: drinks are great in general. But the drinks we ordered last night — those specific ones — get the.
알렉스|en|Three cases, three choices. Practice noticing which case you are in each time.
민지|en|Perfect. Now, Correction Clinic time. Quick rounds. Say the correct version out loud if you can.
알렉스|en|You said: I can't win through my desire.
민지|en|Better: I can't resist my cravings.
알렉스|en|Cravings are things you resist, give in to, satisfy, or fight. The Korean 이기다 means win, but English picks a more specific verb.
민지|en|Collocations matter. Certain verbs go with certain nouns. Resist a temptation. Overcome a fear. Satisfy a hunger.
알렉스|en|You said: Today's topic is what you go to drink, right?
민지|en|Better: Today's topic is about go-to drinks, right?
알렉스|en|Go-to is a compound adjective. It comes before the noun. My go-to coffee. Her go-to gym. Their go-to movie.
민지|en|Practice sentence: What's your go-to drink when you need energy? Try it out loud.
알렉스|en|You said: I was wondering about go-to is the usual expression?
민지|en|Better: I was wondering if go-to is a commonly used expression?
알렉스|en|After wonder, know, ask, tell me — for yes-no questions, use if or whether. Not about plus a full sentence.
민지|ko|wonder about 다음에는 명사구가 와야 해요. I wonder about the price. 문장 전체를 연결하려면 I wonder IF 또는 WHETHER를 쓰세요.
알렉스|en|More examples: I didn't know if she was coming. Can you tell me whether this seat is taken? I was wondering if we could meet tomorrow.
민지|en|Last one in the clinic. You said: Because that is about to respect the mind.
알렉스|en|Better: Because it's a sign of respect.
민지|en|Fixed phrase: a sign of respect. Also very useful: out of respect. We bow out of respect. We listen out of respect.
알렉스|en|Great work in the Correction Clinic. Now, a quick pronunciation tip.
민지|en|Did you know the word the has two different sounds? Before a consonant sound: say thuh. Before a vowel sound: say thee.
알렉스|en|Thuh dog. Thuh book. Thuh chair. Consonant sounds: thuh.
민지|en|Thee apple. Thee ocean. Thee evening. Vowel sounds: thee.
알렉스|en|And when you want to emphasize something special, you can say thee even before a consonant. She is thee restaurant to visit in Seoul.
민지|en|It signals: this is THE one. The best. The most important. A useful little trick.
알렉스|en|Now, Vocabulary in Context. Words are alive. Let's see how they actually live.
민지|en|Word one: spontaneously. Without planning. We decided to try the new place spontaneously.
알렉스|en|You can also say: on a whim, on the spur of the moment. Same warm, free feeling.
민지|en|Word two: craving. A strong desire, especially for food or drink. I have a serious craving for ramen right now.
알렉스|en|Key collocations: have a craving, satisfy a craving, resist a craving, give in to your craving. Notice each verb changes the story.
민지|en|Word three: go-to. Your reliable favorite. What's your go-to order at a coffee shop? Mine is always Americano.
알렉스|en|Word four: ambiance. The atmosphere or feeling of a place. The restaurant had a wonderful ambiance. In casual speech: the vibe was great.
민지|en|Adjectives that go with ambiance: cozy, romantic, lively, relaxed. The coffee shop had a cozy ambiance. Try describing a place you know.
알렉스|en|Word five: culinary. Related to food and cooking. A culinary adventure. Her culinary skills are amazing.
민지|en|Bonus word: sober. It means not drunk, clear-headed. I'm staying sober tonight. Also used to mean serious or calm. A sober decision.
알렉스|en|And heavyweight versus lightweight. We used these for alcohol, but you can use them for anything. She's a heavyweight in the industry. He's a lightweight in chess.
민지|en|One extra word: hooked on. I'm hooked on this podcast. I'm hooked on night walks. Whatever you love and keep coming back to.
알렉스|en|Now, Topic Talk. This week's theme was drinks and drinking culture. Five expressions for real conversations.
민지|en|One: I'm a lightweight. Gets drunk very easily. I had one glass and I was completely done. Total lightweight.
알렉스|en|Two: I can hold my liquor. The opposite. Not easily affected by alcohol. She can really hold her liquor.
민지|en|Three: I'm hooked on this. You love something and can't stop. I'm hooked on sparkling water lately.
알렉스|en|Four: I've developed a taste for it. You used to dislike it, now you enjoy it. I've developed a taste for dry red wine.
민지|en|Five: it hits differently. Something affects you in a special, powerful way. Hot soup on a cold rainy day just hits differently.
알렉스|en|Pick one of those five and say it out loud at least once today. Even to yourself in the mirror.
민지|en|Now, our favorite part. Culture Corner. Every week, a story that makes English more interesting.
알렉스|en|This week: the mysterious saying, break a leg. Why do we say this to wish someone good luck?
민지|en|First time you hear it, it sounds terrible. You're nervous before a big speech and your friend says: break a leg!
알렉스|en|That sounds like a curse! Break your leg? That's the worst thing you could say before a performance!
민지|en|But it actually means the exact opposite. Good luck, go for it, you'll be amazing.
알렉스|en|The most popular theory comes from old theatre culture. In 16th and 17th century theatres, the side curtains on stage were called legs.
민지|en|Actors waited backstage, behind those curtains. Only the performers chosen to go on stage that night would cross through them.
알렉스|en|So to say break a leg meant: I hope you get to cross that curtain tonight. I hope you get to perform.
민지|ko|'leg'는 무대 옆 커튼이었어요. 그 커튼을 가로질러야 무대에 설 수 있었죠. 그래서 '커튼을 꺾어라' = '무대에 서게 되길 바란다'는 행운의 말이 됐다는 거예요.
알렉스|en|Another theory: when actors received big applause, they bowed so deeply that they bent the straight line of their leg.
민지|en|So break a leg meant: I hope you bow so many times tonight because the audience loves you.
알렉스|en|Today the phrase is used before any big moment. Exams, job interviews, presentations, sports competitions.
민지|en|And here is a key cultural note: never say good luck to an actor before a show. In theatre culture, it is considered bad luck.
알렉스|en|Always say break a leg instead. Now you know. And next time someone is nervous, you can say it with confidence.
민지|en|Languages carry their history in every phrase. That history is what makes them alive.
